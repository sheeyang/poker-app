# M4-10 implementation evidence

Online Tables now play continuously. Every Online Table enforces its Action Time Limit with exact Action Deadlines: a missed deadline plays the Timeout Action, later missed decisions in the Hand time out too, and the same commit records an after-Hand sit-out for the human, so they sit out once the payout phase ends and resume only by asking. After the payout phase a 3 s `pause` Presentation Phase follows. When it ends, and once the Hand's after-Hand requests have been applied, the server announces a 5 s Next-Hand Countdown (`schedule.nextHandCountdown`) if at least two Eligible Participants remain, at least one of them a connected human. At its expiry one committed transition sits out the humans still disconnected, then deals (`deal:<Hand ID>`) or cancels the countdown (`no-deal:<session revision>`). A commit or connection change that lets the table deal announces a fresh one. Deal is never accepted at an Online Table.

A human is connected while at least one live connection of their Guest is subscribed to the table. Views of Online Tables carry `connectedParticipantIds`, and each change is published to everyone as a transient `table-presence` message; nothing is persisted, so after a restart every human counts as disconnected until they reconnect. A failed Online Bot decision is retried once with identical inputs and seeds. If the retry fails too, the failure is shown to everyone, and the server plays the Bot's Timeout Action and sits it out after the Hand. Practice Tables keep explicit Retry, which an Online Table refuses. Persisted countdowns survive restarts; one that expired during an outage runs its recheck at startup. The behavior is described in the [server protocol reference](../server-protocol.md#automatic-dealing-at-online-tables).

Server tests use the manual clock, several sockets per Guest and restarts on one schema. `apps/server/test/automatic-dealing.test.ts` covers the announcement and its expiry, everyone seeing the countdown, the payout phase and pause between Hands, the expiry recheck (a disconnected human sat out, a cancelled countdown, a fresh countdown after a reconnection and after an explicit resume), public and transient connection status across two devices of one Guest and a restart, the Online Action Deadline with every later missed decision timing out and the after-Hand sit-out, a human who disconnects mid-Hand and returns in time, a Guest seated mid-Hand dealt in next Hand, a countdown kept across a restart, one that expired during an outage, a deadline that expired during an outage (exactly one Timeout Action, one Hand start, chips conserved), and a deal whose commit failed once. `apps/server/test/online-bot-failures.test.ts` covers the one retry with identical inputs and seeds, and the Timeout Action, sit-out and shared failure after a second failure. The Online parts of `online-tables`, `participant-authorization` and `after-hand-requests` now deal through Automatic Dealing instead of the earlier stored-Hand stand-in. Pure web tests in `apps/web/test/presentation.test.ts` and `apps/web/test/online-status.test.ts` cover the countdown, its ticks and labels, connection status, and what the table is waiting for.

## Browser check

On 2 November 2026, using the [schedule browser fixture](../local-development.md#schedule-browser-fixture) (system clock, automatic Bots; API port 3140, control port 6040, Vite port 5240) and two isolated Chrome sessions, one per Guest. Ann created a 3-seat Online Table with a 15 s limit and one Bot, and Ben joined through the link.

- Ben's Table options listed Ann as "Connected". Once both humans were seated the first Hand was dealt automatically. Neither acted in time, so each Timeout Action folded, and each human was then shown as "Sitting out" with "Return to play". Meanwhile the felt and the tray read "Waiting for two players ready to play, one of them a connected person". Once Ann seated the Bot, the next Hand was dealt to Ben and the Bot without anyone pressing Deal.
- Ben's own Action Deadline showed as "10s to act" and "Your turn · 10s"; Ann's deadline showed on Ben's screen as "Thinking… · 15s". After both returned to play, an in-page helper clicked the real Check/Call buttons as they enabled, and Hands 6 to 16 were dealt in succession. Between Hands, Ben's Trainer read "Next hand coming up" during the pause, then "Next hand in 5s", "Next hand in 4s"…. The 6,000 chips at the table were conserved throughout.
- Stopping Ben's browser marked his seat "Disconnected" on Ann's screen (dashed and dimmed), and the server's view listed only Ann. Navigating Ben's headless tab to `about:blank` alone had not ended the connection; the browser apparently kept the page's socket open.
- `POST /restart` restarted the server mid-Hand. Ann's Trainer reconnected by itself, and the table carried on from its persisted state with chips conserved. Her Call, sent while the server was down, stayed pending as "Waiting for confirmation" (the existing command retention), so her deadline passed and she was sat out after the Hand, as the outage rules allow until M9.
- The Online Bot failure, retry and Timeout Action were not exercised in the browser (the fixture's Bots do not fail); the server tests cover them.
