# Keep PokerCore Internal Until a Second Ruleset Exists

The engine package will expose a deep stateful CashGame module while keeping PokerCore as an internal stateful module for generic card, chip, contribution, pot, and conservation mechanisms. With only one concrete ruleset, a public Core seam would make callers orchestrate low-level mechanics without demonstrated reuse; a second real ruleset may justify extracting a public interface after its genuinely shared behavior is known.

CashGame owns Texas Hold'em eligibility, buy-ins and top-ups, antes and blinds, betting legality and reopening, dealing and burns, hand evaluation, settlement, and observability. PokerCore owns authoritative card locations and deck state, participant and seat structure, chip stacks, contribution ledgers, derived pot layers, and invariant-preserving transfers. Its stateful mechanisms may commit or return chips, draw or move cards, derive pots, and apply payouts; they do not decide why a contribution occurs, whether a poker action is legal, how a board is dealt, or which hand wins.

The engine may expose its pure Texas Hold'em hand-ranking helper to trusted server-side consumers so Research equity calculations reuse the same ranking implementation as CashGame settlement. The helper evaluates explicitly supplied cards without reading CashGame or PokerCore state; this does not make PokerCore public or move dealing, visibility, or payout decisions out of CashGame.

Do not put rule-level operations such as `call`, `bet`, `raise`, `postBlind`, `postAnte`, `dealFlop`, or `runShowdown` on PokerCore. CashGame uses encapsulated Core methods rather than directly editing stacks, contributions, or card collections, so related state changes preserve card uniqueness and chip conservation together. The internal Core remains independently testable without becoming a public orchestration API; the agreed cash-game behavior is recorded in [ADR-0025](0025-document-standard-cash-game-rules.md).
